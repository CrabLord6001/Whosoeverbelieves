About these texts

Each column is a printed edition, not a single manuscript. Spelling conventions that carry no difference in meaning — the movable nu (ἐγέννησε / ἐγέννησεν), elision, accents, and capitalization — are treated as the same reading and are not marked.

  • Textus Receptus — F. H. A. Scrivener, The New Testament in the Original Greek according to the Text followed in the Authorised Version (Cambridge, 1894). Public domain.
  • Byzantine Majority Text — Maurice A. Robinson and William G. Pierpont, The New Testament in the Original Greek: Byzantine Textform (2018). Released into the public domain by the editors.
  • SBLGNT — Michael W. Holmes, ed., The Greek New Testament: SBL Edition (Society of Biblical Literature and Logos Bible Software, 2010). Licensed CC BY 4.0. About 98.5% agreement with the Westcott–Hort (1881), where both are considered Critical Text.

Reading the parsing codes

Hovering a highlighted word shows its dictionary form, its meaning, and a parsing code. The three editions here ship two different coding systems — Robinson’s and MorphGNT’s — which disagree on real points, so both have been converted to the one scheme below. The code is often the whole story behind a variant: roughly half of the differences between these texts leave the English unchanged, and in those the parsing is what actually differs.

  • V: verb
  • Adj: adjective
  • Art: article
  • Adv: adverb
  • Prep: preposition
  • Conj: conjunction
  • Prtcl: particle
  • Cond: conditional
  • PPro: personal
  • RelPro: relative
  • DemPro: demonstrative
  • IntPro: interrogative
  • IndefPro: indefinite
  • CorrPro: correlative
  • RefPro: reflexive
  • PossPro: possessive
  • Interj: interjection

  • G: genitive

  • V: vocative
  • S: singular
  • P: plural

Verbs: tense · voice · mood
  • P: present
  • I: imperfect
  • F: future
  • A: aorist
  • R: perfect
  • L: pluperfect
  • A: active

  • P: passive

So N-ASF is a noun, accusative singular feminine. A finite verb then carries person and number (V-PAI-3S, present active indicative, third person singular); a participle or infinitive carries case, number and gender instead (V-RPP-ASF, perfect passive participle, accusative singular feminine).

About the English

Each column carries a translation made from its own family of text, so the English moves with the Greek above it rather than against it. The fit is not equally tight in all three. The King James Version sits closest: Scrivener assembled his Greek text to match the readings the KJV translators followed, so the two correspond almost by construction. The World English Bible New Testament rests on the Majority Text. The American Standard Version (1901) was made from the critical text of its own day, not from the SBLGNT, so it reflects the critical tradition broadly but will not follow every editorial decision Holmes made. All three are public domain.

Word meanings shown on hover come from the dictionary of Greek words in James Strong’s Exhaustive Concordance (1890), which is in the public domain; the machine-readable edition is maintained by Open Scriptures. A gloss gives the general range of a word’s meaning, not the sense it carries in this particular verse, and it is shown only for nouns, verbs, adjectives and adverbs — for articles, pronouns and prepositions only the dictionary form appears, because Strong’s renderings for those are too broad to be useful.

The Nestle–Aland text is not shown. It is under copyright and cannot be reproduced in full; the SBLGNT stands here as the critical text.
