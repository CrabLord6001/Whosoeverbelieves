Genesis opens the whole drama. After the prologue of creation it runs through four of the seven acts, and each one ends with God stepping in: the fall, the flood, Babel, and the call of Abraham.

The Big Idea

Genesis presents the origins behind the founding of the theocratic kingdom, the rule of God through human mediators over all creation. It focuses on the promised blessing of the seed in the land.

Philippe R. Sterling

What the numbers show

Three lives from Adam to Isaac

Adam was alive for 243 years of Methuselah’s life, Methuselah for 98 years of Shem’s, and Shem for the first 50 years of Isaac’s. The story of creation could pass from Adam to Abraham’s family through only three men.

Methuselah and the flood

Methuselah died in year 1656, the year the flood came. Lamech, Noah’s father, died five years before it (Genesis 5:30–31; 7:6).

Life grows shorter

Before the flood men lived about 900 years. Within ten generations after it, lifespans fall below 200, and by Moses’ day “seventy years, or even by reason of strength eighty” (Psalm 90:10).

Eber outlived Abraham

Eber, from whose name “Hebrew” probably comes (Genesis 10:21), died four years after Abraham. Noah died just two years before Abraham was born.

Timeline

Abraham’s Walk of Faith

His life by his age, where Genesis gives it. Gold marks the high points of his faith; red marks the times he relied on his own plans.

  1.  
    Ur

    Called in Mesopotamia

    God first appeared to Abraham in Ur, before he lived in Haran (Acts 7:2–4). His father Terah led the family as far as Haran (Genesis 11:31).

  2. 75years old
    Haran

    “Leave your country”

    God promises a land, a great nation and blessing for all the families of the earth. Abram goes, “not knowing where he went” (Genesis 12:1–4; Hebrews 11:8).

  3. 75years old
    Shechem and Bethel

    Altars in the land

    At Shechem Yahweh promises, “I will give this land to your offspring,” and Abram builds altars there and near Bethel (Genesis 12:6–8).

  4.  
    Egypt

    Fear in a famine

    Abram goes down to Egypt and calls Sarai his sister to save himself. God protects her anyway (Genesis 12:10–20).

  5.  
    Bethel to Hebron

    Lot chooses Sodom

    Abram lets Lot choose first. Lot picks the well-watered plain near Sodom, and God renews the promise of the land to Abram (Genesis 13:8–18).

  6.  
    Dan and Salem

    The rescue of Lot

    With 318 trained men Abram pursues the kings as far as Dan and rescues Lot. Melchizedek, king of Salem and priest of God Most High, blesses him (Genesis 14:14–20).

  7.  
    Hebron

    “He believed in Yahweh”

    God promises offspring as many as the stars, and “he believed in Yahweh, who credited it to him for righteousness” (Genesis 15:5–6). Paul builds on this verse: Abraham was declared righteous by faith (Romans 4:3).

  8. 85years old
    Hebron

    Sarai’s plan

    After ten years in Canaan with no child, Sarai gives Hagar to Abram (Genesis 16:1–3).

  9. 86years old
    Beer Lahai Roi

    Ishmael is born

    Hagar flees, and the angel of Yahweh meets her in the wilderness. She calls Him “a God who sees” (Genesis 16:13; 16:16). See below.

  10. 99years old
    Mamre

    A new name and a sign

    God changes Abram’s name to Abraham, “father of a multitude,” gives circumcision as the sign of the covenant, and promises a son through Sarah (Genesis 17:5; 17:24). “Is anything too hard for Yahweh?” (Genesis 18:14)

  11. 99years old
    Mamre

    Pleading for Sodom

    Abraham intercedes for the city, and God rescues Lot before judgment falls (Genesis 18:23–33; 19:29).

  12.  
    Gerar

    The same fear again

    Abraham again says of Sarah, “She is my sister,” and again God protects her (Genesis 20:2).

  13. 100years old
    Beersheba

    Isaac is born

    The promised son arrives (Genesis 21:5). Hagar and Ishmael are sent away, and “God heard the voice of the boy” (Genesis 21:17).

  14.  
    Moriah

    “God will provide”

    God tests Abraham by asking for Isaac. Abraham believes God can raise the dead, and God provides a ram (Genesis 22:8; 22:14; Hebrews 11:17–19). Moriah is where the temple was later built (2 Chronicles 3:1).

  15. 137years old
    Hebron

    Sarah dies

    Sarah dies at 127, and Abraham buys the cave of Machpelah, his only land in Canaan (Genesis 23:1–20).

  16. 140years old
    Hebron

    A wife for Isaac

    Abraham’s servant brings Rebekah from his relatives in Paddan-aram (Genesis 24:1–4; Genesis 25:20).

  17. 175years old
    Machpelah

    Abraham dies

    Isaac and Ishmael bury him beside Sarah (Genesis 25:7–9).

Hagar and the God Who Sees

Genesis 16 in four steps. Abram and Sarai try to bring about God’s promise their own way, and an Egyptian servant becomes the first person in Scripture to meet the angel of Yahweh.

1

Sarai’s plan

“It may be that I will obtain children by her” (Genesis 16:2). Sarai gives her Egyptian servant Hagar to Abram, and Hagar conceives.

2

Hagar flees

Hagar looks down on Sarai, Sarai treats her harshly, and Hagar runs toward Egypt on the road to Shur (16:4–7).

3

The angel of Yahweh

“Where did you come from? Where are you going?” He sends her back and promises a son: Ishmael, “because Yahweh has heard your affliction” (16:8–12).

4

“A God who sees”

Hagar names Him “a God who sees,” and the well is called Beer Lahai Roi (16:13–14). Abram is 86 when Ishmael is born (16:16).

Years later God hears the boy again in the wilderness (Genesis 21:17). Paul uses Hagar and Sarah to picture two covenants: slavery under the Law and freedom by promise (Galatians 4:22–26).

Timeline

The Messianic Thread

Genesis plants the promise of a Redeemer and keeps narrowing where He will come from.

  1. 3:15

    The seed of the woman

    A long conflict between two seeds and the final victory of the seed of the woman. Galatians 4:4–5 identifies the seed as the Son of God, born of a woman.

  2. 5:22–24

    Enoch

    Jude 14–15 records Enoch’s prophecy that the Lord will come with ten thousands of His holy ones to judge.

  3. 9:25–27

    The blessing of Shem

    The blessings lost at the fall will come through the line of Shem.

  4. 12:1–3

    The promises to Abraham

    All the nations will be blessed through Abraham’s seed, fulfilled in Christ (Galatians 3:16). Abraham will be the father of kings (Genesis 17:6), and the promise is repeated in Genesis 22:16–18.

  5. 14:18–20

    Melchizedek

    A type of Christ, the eternal king-priest (Hebrews 7:1–3).

  6. 49:10

    The king from Judah

    The ruler will come from Judah and bring an age of abundance (49:8–12). Revelation 5:5 applies this to Christ.

Notes & Sources

  1. The Hebrew word tôlĕdôt (“generations”) heads each section. It announces a starting point and could be translated “this is what became of…”.
  2. Lifespans follow the Hebrew (Masoretic) text. The Greek Septuagint adds about 100 years to most fathers’ ages in Genesis 5 and 11 and includes a second Cainan, whom Luke 3:36 also names. Shem was born when Noah was 502: he was 100 “two years after the flood” (Genesis 11:10), which came in Noah’s 600th year (Genesis 7:6).
  3. Abraham is placed in year 2008, born when Terah was 130. Terah died at 205 in Haran (Genesis 11:32), and Abraham left Haran at 75 after his father’s death (Genesis 12:4; Acts 7:4). Read alone, Genesis 11:26 could suggest Terah was 70; on that reading every date from Abraham on moves 60 years earlier.
  4. The BC scale counts back from an Exodus in 1446 BC (1 Kings 6:1) and 430 years in Egypt (Exodus 12:40). Jacob entered Egypt at 130 (Genesis 47:9), in year 2298, which gives creation about 4174 BC and Abraham’s birth in 2166 BC. Ussher’s 4004 BC counts only 215 years in Egypt. Many interpreters also allow for gaps in the genealogies.
  5. Ur is placed at Tell el-Muqayyar in southern Iraq, its traditional site. Some scholars propose a northern Ur near Haran. Salem is Jerusalem (Psalm 76:2), and Moriah is the temple mount (2 Chronicles 3:1). The site of Sodom is unknown; it is marked near the south end of the Dead Sea. The route between Haran and Canaan follows the main trade road through Aleppo and Damascus; Genesis does not describe it.
  6. Abraham’s ages come from Genesis 12:4; 16:16; 17:24; 21:5; 23:1 (Sarah was ten years younger, 17:17); 25:7; and 25:20.
  7. The structure follows Allen P. Ross, Creation and Blessing, as adapted in Philippe R. Sterling’s Old Testament survey. The Seven Acts summaries draw on Chafer and Walvoord, Major Bible Themes.
