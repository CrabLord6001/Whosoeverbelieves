Genesis opens the whole drama. After the prologue of creation it runs through four of the seven acts, and each one ends with God stepping in: the fall, the flood, Babel, and the call of Abraham.

The Big Idea

Genesis presents the origins behind the founding of the theocratic kingdom, the rule of God through human mediators over all creation. It focuses on the promised blessing of the seed in the land.

The Line of Promise

Each section of Genesis opens with “These are the generations of…”, which means “this is what became of…”. The bars show how much space each gets. Genesis dispatches the lines that are set aside in a few verses, then slows down to tell the chosen line in full.

What Became of…

The heading of each section names a starting point, and the section tells what became of it.

Generations ofPassageWhat became of it
Prologue1:1–2:3God created the universe, the earth, and humanity and commissioned humanity to fill and rule over the earth.
The heavens and the earth2:4–4:26What became of creation: the fall, the murder of Abel, and sin spreading through an expanding civilization. The promise of the seed begins here (Genesis 3:15).
Adam5:1–6:8A record of death. Everyone dies except Enoch, who walked with God. The section ends with God’s displeasure over humanity, yet a remnant finds grace.
Noah6:9–9:29Judgment in the flood and grace in the deliverance of a remnant. The new age still carries sin, and the promise now focuses on Shem.
Shem, Ham & Japheth10:1–11:9The nations spread from Noah’s sons and are scattered at Babel.
Shem11:10–26A transition that narrows the focus from the line of Shem to Abram.
Terah11:27–25:11What became of Terah through Abraham. The problem of an heir drives the story. Abraham believed the LORD, and it was credited to him as righteousness (Genesis 15:6).
Ishmael25:12–18Not the chosen line, yet blessed as Abraham’s son.
Isaac25:19–35:29Jacob and Esau struggle, and the promise given to Abraham passes to Jacob. The tribes of Israel begin.
Esau36:1–37:1The line of Edom, told because Edom and Amalek (Genesis 36:12) will matter later. Amalek’s story resurfaces in Esther.
Jacob37:2–50:26Joseph and his brothers, and how the tribes came to Egypt. God brings good out of evil, and the book ends waiting for God to visit His people (Genesis 50:24).
Timeline

The Messianic Thread

Genesis plants the promise of a Redeemer and keeps narrowing where He will come from.

  1. 3:15

    The seed of the woman

    A long conflict between two seeds and the final victory of the seed of the woman. Galatians 4:4–5 identifies the seed as the Son of God, born of a woman.

  2. 5:22–24

    Enoch

    Jude 14–15 records Enoch’s prophecy that the Lord will come with ten thousands of His holy ones to judge.

  3. 9:25–27

    The blessing of Shem

    The blessings lost at the fall will come through the line of Shem.

  4. 12:1–3

    The promises to Abraham

    All the nations will be blessed through Abraham’s seed, fulfilled in Christ (Galatians 3:16). Abraham will be the father of kings (Genesis 17:6), and the promise is repeated in Genesis 22:16–18.

  5. 14:18–20

    Melchizedek

    A type of Christ, the eternal king-priest (Hebrews 7:1–3).

  6. 49:10

    The king from Judah

    The ruler will come from Judah and bring an age of abundance (49:8–12). Revelation 5:5 applies this to Christ.

Notes & Sources

  1. The Hebrew word tôlĕdôt (“generations”) heads each section. It announces a starting point and could be translated “this is what became of…”.
  2. Verse counts are from the World English Bible. Genesis has 1,533 verses; the three sections of the chosen line fill 1,189 of them.
  3. The structure follows Allen P. Ross, Creation and Blessing, as adapted in Philippe R. Sterling’s Old Testament survey. The Seven Acts summaries draw on Chafer and Walvoord, Major Bible Themes.
