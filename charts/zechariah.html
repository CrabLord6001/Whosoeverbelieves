Zechariah preached in Act 5, after the return from Babylon, to encourage the people to finish the temple (Ezra 5:1–2; 6:14). His last chapters look far ahead: to the King’s first coming, His rejection, and His return to reign over the earth in Act 7. See where he fits among the Persian kings in Esther in Context.

At a Glance

WhoZechariah, a priest and prophet who preached alongside Haggai, with Joshua the high priest and Zerubbabel the governor1:1
WhatEight night visions, four messages and two burdens that urge the returned exiles to finish the temple and look ahead to the coming King
WhereJerusalem, among the exiles who had come home from BabylonEzra 5:1
When520–518 BC under Darius I for chapters 1–8; chapters 9–14 probably after 480 BC
WhyGod has not forgotten His people. He will cleanse Israel and send the Messiah, the humble King on a donkey who will one day reign over all the earth9:9
HowTwo halves: dated visions and messages for the present (1–8), then two undated burdens about the King rejected and the King reigning (9–14)

Timeline

The Eight Night Visions

All eight visions came in one night (Zechariah 1:8), three months after the work on the temple resumed.

  1. 11:7–17

    Rider among the myrtle trees

    God is angry with the nations at ease and has returned to Jerusalem with mercy. “My house shall be built in it” (1:16).

  2. 21:18–21

    Four horns and four craftsmen

    The powers that scattered Judah will themselves be cast down.

  3. 32:1–13

    A man with a measuring line

    Jerusalem will overflow its walls, and Yahweh will be a wall of fire around her (2:5).

  4. 43:1–10

    Joshua the high priest

    Satan accuses, but God removes the priest’s filthy garments: a picture of Israel cleansed. “I will bring out my servant, the Branch” (3:8).

  5. 54:1–14

    The golden lampstand and two olive trees

    The temple will be finished, “not by might, nor by power, but by my Spirit” (4:6).

  6. 65:1–4

    The flying scroll

    God’s curse goes out against thieves and those who swear falsely.

  7. 75:5–11

    The woman in the basket

    Wickedness is shut in a measuring basket and carried off to Shinar (Babylon).

  8. 86:1–8

    Four chariots

    God’s judgment patrols the whole earth, and His Spirit is at rest.

  9. ★6:9–15
    The climax

    The Branch crowned

    A crown is set on Joshua the high priest as a sign of the coming Branch, who “will be a priest on his throne” (Zechariah 6:13). In Israel the offices of king and priest were kept apart; the Messiah will hold both (Psalm 110:4; Hebrews 7:1–3).

Notes & Sources

  1. Dates are reckoned from Darius I’s reign: his second year runs from spring 520 to spring 519 BC, and his fourth year from 518 to 517 BC.
  2. Chapters 9–14 carry no date. The mention of Greece as a rising power (Zechariah 9:13) leads many conservative scholars to date them after 480 BC, late in Zechariah’s life. Critical scholars often assign them to a later “Second Zechariah,” but the book presents itself as one prophet’s work.
  3. Matthew 27:9–10 credits the thirty pieces of silver to “Jeremiah.” Matthew is likely combining Zechariah 11:12–13 with Jeremiah’s potter and field (Jeremiah 19:1–2; 32:6–9) and naming the better-known prophet.
  4. Word counts are from the World English Bible, which renders “the LORD of hosts” as “Yahweh of Armies.”
  5. The outline of the book is adapted from Irving L. Jensen’s survey chart of Zechariah.
