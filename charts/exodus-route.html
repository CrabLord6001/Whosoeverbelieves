The Exodus closes Act 4, the Age of Promise. God keeps His word to Abraham and brings his descendants out of Egypt. At Sinai He gives the Law, and Act 5, the Age of Israel under the Law, begins.

A Warning for Believers

The generation that left Egypt had been redeemed by the blood of the Passover lamb, yet through unbelief they did not enter the land (Hebrews 3:19). Hebrews uses them to warn believers that they can fall short of God’s rest (Hebrews 4:1). What is at stake is the believer’s inheritance and reward, not eternal life.

The Forty Years

Moses dates many events by the month and year after leaving Egypt. Red entries mark the unbelief at Kadesh and the years it cost.

  1. 1446 BCMonth 1, day 15
    Exodus

    Israel leaves Egypt

    After 430 years in Egypt, Israel sets out from Rameses the day after the Passover.

    Exodus 12:40–41; Numbers 33:3
  2.  Month 1
    Red Sea

    Crossing the sea

    God leads them by the wilderness road rather than the coastal road, and opens the sea before them.

    Exodus 13:17–18; 14:21–22
  3.  Month 2, day 15
    Wilderness of Sin

    Manna from heaven

    Between Elim and Sinai the people grumble for food, and God sends quail and manna.

    Exodus 16:1; 16:13–15
  4.  Month 3
    Mount Sinai

    The Law is given

    Israel camps before the mountain. God gives the Ten Commandments and makes His covenant with the nation.

    Exodus 19:1–2; 20:1–17
  5. 1445 BCYear 2, month 1, day 1
    Mount Sinai

    The tabernacle set up

    Exodus 40:17
  6. 1445 BCYear 2, month 2, day 20

Notes & Sources

  1. The date of 1446 BC comes from 1 Kings 6:1: Solomon began the temple in 966 BC, “the four hundred and eightieth year” after the Exodus. Forty years later Israel entered the land in 1406 BC.
  2. Most of these places cannot be located with certainty. The map uses the traditional identifications: Succoth at Tell el-Maskhuta, Marah at ‘Ain Hawarah, Elim at Wadi Gharandel, Rephidim at Wadi Feiran, Hazeroth at ‘Ain Khadra, and Kadesh Barnea at ‘Ain el-Qudeirat.
  3. Mount Sinai is placed at Jebel Musa, its traditional site since the fourth century. Other proposals include Jebel Sin Bishar, Har Karkom in the Negev, and Jebel al-Lawz in Arabia.
  4. The Hebrew Yam Suph (“Red Sea,” or “Sea of Reeds”) may refer to the lakes along the modern Suez Canal, the Gulf of Suez, or the Gulf of Aqaba. The crossing is drawn at the head of the Gulf of Suez.
  5. Mount Hor is shown at its traditional site near Petra. Some place it closer to Kadesh, at Jebel Madurah.
  6. Coastlines, rivers, and lakes are from Natural Earth (public domain). The modern Suez Canal is not shown.
