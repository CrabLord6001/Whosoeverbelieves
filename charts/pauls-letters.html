Paul’s letters belong to Act 6, the Age of the Church. They explain the mystery that was not made known in earlier generations: Jews and Gentiles joined in one body in Christ (Ephesians 3:5–6).

Paul and the Corinthians

Paul’s letters and visits to Corinth can be pieced together from Acts and the two letters. Red entries are a letter we no longer have or a visit Acts does not record.

  1. 1

    First visit

    Paul founds the church on his second journey and stays eighteen months.

    Acts 18:1–11
  2. 2

    A previous letter (lost)

    Paul tells the church not to associate with immoral believers.

    1 Corinthians 5:9
  3. 3

    First Corinthians

    Written from Ephesus on the third journey about problems reported to him and questions the church sent.

    1 Corinthians 16:8
  4. 4

    A painful visit

    Paul visits but fails to accomplish his purpose.

    2 Corinthians 2:1; 13:1–2
  5. 5

    A severe letter (lost)

    A “sorrowful” letter sent by Titus.

    2 Corinthians 2:4
  6. 6

    Second Corinthians

    Titus meets Paul in Macedonia with a good report, and Paul writes in response.

    2 Corinthians 7:5–13
  7. 7

    Third visit

    Paul likely visits Corinth near the close of the third journey.

    Acts 20:1–3

Notes & Sources

  1. Dates follow a common conservative chronology and are approximate. The Gallio inscription at Delphi places Paul in Corinth around AD 51–52 (Acts 18:12).
  2. Galatians is dated here before the Jerusalem Council, taking “Galatia” as the Roman province where Paul founded churches on his first journey. Those who read it as the northern region date it around AD 55.
  3. Paul’s release after two years in Rome and a later second imprisonment are inferred from the Pastoral Letters and early church tradition. Acts ends before that time.
  4. Hebrews does not name its author, so it is not included here.
  5. For the routes themselves, see the map of Paul’s Missionary Journeys.
