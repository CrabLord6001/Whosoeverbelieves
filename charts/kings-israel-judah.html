The Big Idea

1 and 2 Kings record the failure of the theocracy under monarchy. The kings of Israel and Judah could not righteously rule themselves and the nation.

Philippe R. Sterling

The Kings of Israel

Every king of the northern kingdom is said to have done evil, most of them by following “the sins of Jeroboam” (1 Kings 15:34). Nine different families held the throne, often by murder.

The Kings of Judah

Judah kept one dynasty, David’s, for its whole history, as God had promised (2 Samuel 7:16). Athaliah’s six years were the only break, and even then a son of David was hidden in the temple (2 Kings 11:2–3).

Notes & Sources

  1. Dates follow Edwin R. Thiele, The Mysterious Numbers of the Hebrew Kings, the chronology used by most evangelical study Bibles. Thiele’s overlaps explain why the reign lengths in Kings add up to more years than the calendar allows.
  2. Tibni ruled part of Israel as a rival to Omri for about five years (1 Kings 16:21–22). He is not shown on the chart.
  3. Jehu is called evil because he kept Jeroboam’s golden calves, though God commended him for destroying Baal worship (2 Kings 10:30–31).
  4. Prophets’ dates are approximate and taken from the kings named in their books. Joel and Obadiah are left off because their dates are uncertain.
  5. Manasseh repented late in life (2 Chronicles 33:12–13), but Kings judges his reign as the worst in Judah’s history.
